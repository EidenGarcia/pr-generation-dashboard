Frequency against IEEE limits

The grid runs at 60 Hz. The further frequency strays and the longer it stays away, the closer generators and inverters get to tripping off. Each 5-minute reading from PREPA is placed in a level below; any reading outside Normal starts an excursion, logged with its duration and whether it outlasted the time allowed at that level.

Last 24 hours on the limit scale

The scale stretches the area near 60 Hz so small swings stay visible while the IEEE limits remain on the chart. Dashed lines are IEEE 1547-2018 trip settings.

Levels and where they come from

    Readings are 5 minutes apart, so short dips between readings can be missed and durations are estimates. “Possibly exceeded” means a single reading was outside the range and the event could have lasted longer than allowed.

    Live data is read directly from PREPA every 5 minutes while this page is open, and every 15 minutes by a GitHub Action that keeps the outage log, the daily summary text logs, frequency excursions and portal health. Sources: PREPA operational data — dataSource.js (plants, units, metrics) and dataGraph.js (24-hour generation), checked hourly. Units below 1 MW count as offline; the feed does not say whether a unit is on outage, maintenance or reserve. Frequency levels: IEEE 1547-2018 continuous-operation range and default trip settings, generator continuous-operation limits used in WECC and AESO practice, and a ±0.2 Hz normal range set in data/frequency_limits.json. The forecast is this dashboard’s own estimate, not PREPA’s. Unit rankings use Genera’s quarterly filings in PREB docket NEPR-MI-2019-0007.
